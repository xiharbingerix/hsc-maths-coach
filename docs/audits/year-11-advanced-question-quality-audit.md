# Year 11 Advanced Question-Quality Audit

**Audit date:** 30 July 2026
**Scope:** all live `year-11-advanced` lesson questions, multi-part questions,
course-scoped challenge questions, and the Year 11 Advanced exam paper.
**Standard applied:** `QUESTION_AUTHORING_STANDARD.md`,
`PRACTICE_QUESTION_STANDARD.md`, and `FEEDBACK_AND_HINTS_STANDARD.md`.
**Mode:** baseline audit followed by unit-by-unit remediation and live verification.

## Final verdict

**FAIL — the course is not publication-ready under the current Question Authoring
Standard.**

The course has broad fluency coverage, reliable visual payloads, and a small set of
strong high-difficulty questions. However, the standard lesson sequence is dominated
by procedural execution and recognition. The mastery layer does not provide the
required progression into analytical, problem-solving, investigative, transfer, and
synthesis tasks.

This is a systemic design issue rather than a small set of isolated bad questions:

* all 126 seeded lessons were inspected;
* all 2,368 collected question-bank rows were included in whole-population checks;
* all 23 D4-D6 rows were manually reviewed;
* exact duplicates, explanation depth, response format, difficulty, visual coverage,
  rendering, and mastery-task mix were checked across the full course; and
* representative high-risk mastery sets were manually reviewed across every unit
  family.

## Finding 1 — mastery is procedural practice presented as mastery

The Practice Question Standard requires mastery to be dominated by production,
transfer, interpretation, modelling, error diagnosis, and connected reasoning. It
also requires a D3 → D4 → D5 progression.

The actual mastery pool contains:

* **7 D4 questions;**
* **0 D5 questions;**
* **0 D6 questions.**

No lesson meets the required D3/D4/D5 mastery profile.

Examples:

* `degrees-and-radians-concept` uses nine recognition-style prompts in its ten-question
  mastery set: state a standard radian value, identify a quadrant, recall a boundary,

* `graphing-exponential-logarithmic-functions` uses eight MCQs in mastery and is
  dominated by recognising intercepts, asymptotes, domains, ranges, and memorised

  sustained proof strategy.

These are legitimate fluency or early-consolidation questions. They are not
mathematically rich mastery tasks.

## Finding 2 — the required task diversity is largely absent

Across the 2,290 standard questions, screening found:

* 830 prompts beginning with a direct procedural command such as calculate, find,
  solve, evaluate, simplify, differentiate, or integrate;
* 479 recognition-style prompts;
* only 119 prompts containing even a surface cue for comparison, interpretation,
  justification, modelling, error analysis, construction, or synthesis; and
* no genuine investigative task after manual review of every apparent
  investigation-pattern match.

The apparent “investigative” matches were ordinary uses of the word *pattern*, such
as identifying a scatter-plot pattern or choosing an algebra pattern. None asked
students to explore cases, make and test a conjecture, or generalise a result.

The standard question sequence also contains no genuine guided-discovery arc. The 33
multi-part rows are mostly decomposed procedural chains—for example, identifying two
derivatives before substituting into a supplied product-rule result. They scaffold
execution but do not guide students to discover a mathematical relationship.

Open tasks are not required in the auto-marked question bank, and unconstrained open
answers would conflict with markability. However, there is currently no optional
open/investigative activity layer to provide the creativity and exploration recognised
by the authoring standard.

## Finding 6 — markability and answer leakage need remediation

### Ambiguous question

`y11adv-cir-m10` asks:

> A circle passes through (0, 11). Find its radius.

No centre is specified. Infinitely many circles pass through that point, so the
authored answer `11` is not uniquely determined. This fails Markability = 5.

### Numbers leaking the answer

The lesson auditor reports two `prompt-reveals-answer` warnings:

* `y11adv-cir-m10` — the intended radius equals the stated coordinate value 11; and
* `y11adv-rat-i2` — a 45° right-triangle setup produces horizontal distance 25 m,
  equal to the stated cliff height.

The second question remains mathematically solvable, but the chosen numbers weaken its
diagnostic power and violate the non-leaking-number guidance.

### Accepted variants

The lesson auditor reports:

* 243 `typed-answer-no-variants` warnings; and
* 2 `multipart-accepted-answer-variants` warnings.

Many simple numeric answers may need no meaningful alternate form, so these warnings
require triage rather than blind addition of duplicate strings. Algebraic,
coordinate, interval, exact-value, and prose answers do require authored variants or
calibrated semantic marking.

The repository-wide answer-marking test also currently fails one case:
`unknown variable not dropped from correct answer`. This is a platform-level failure,
not proven to originate in Year 11 Advanced content, but it reduces confidence in
markability until repaired.

## Finding 7 — diagnostic intent is not auditable

The standard requires a one-line target misconception for every authored question and
a misconception mapping for every MCQ distractor. The current `PracticeQuestion`
shape stores neither:

* a `targetMisconception` field; nor
* a per-distractor misconception/error-method field.

Some MCQs clearly encode useful misconceptions. For example, phase-shift questions
contrast the common horizontal-sign error, and exponential-model questions contrast
`0.05` with `1.05`.

Others are primarily memory tests or use weak distractors. For example,
`y11adv-exp-graph-m7` asks for the approximate value of `e` and offers unrelated
famous constants or arbitrary decimals. A wrong choice does not reliably identify
the next lesson the student needs.

Because mappings are not authored, the formal diagnostic gate cannot be verified at
scale. This should be addressed in authoring metadata or in a sidecar audit manifest,
not inferred after publication.

## Remediation plan

### P0 — correctness, rendering, and markability

1. Rewrite or remove `y11adv-cir-m10`.
2. Change the numbers in `y11adv-rat-i2` so the answer is not copied from the prompt.
3. Fix all 102 scoped unformatted-math findings.
4. Expand all 230 short explanations into question-specific worked reasoning.
5. Triage the 243 typed-answer and 2 multi-part variant warnings.
6. Repair the repository-wide answer-marking test failure.

### P1 — rebuild mastery as mathematically rich assessment

For every lesson:

1. retain appropriate guided and independent fluency;
2. remove exact repeats from mastery;
3. use 2-3 mastery MCQs, with each distractor mapped to a specific wrong method;
4. cap pure recognition at two mastery items;
5. include at least three production questions;
6. include at least two bounded analytical, transfer, modelling, problem-solving,
   investigative, or synthesis tasks;
7. include at least one mathematically specific misconception or invalid-step task;
8. calibrate genuine D4 and D5 tasks by decisions and transfer, not position; and
9. ensure every lesson has 4 guided + 5 independent + 10 mastery questions.

Start with:

* Graph Transformations
* Trigonometry and Measure of Angles
* Trigonometric Identities and Equations
* Exponential and Logarithmic Functions
* Integration
* Exponential and Logarithmic Calculus
* Trigonometric Calculus

### P2 — add explicit task-type diversity

Introduce bounded forms of:

* **problem solving:** method selection without cueing the algorithm;
* **analytical tasks:** compare approaches, classify cases, diagnose an invalid step,
  or interpret a parameter;
* **investigative tasks:** structured case exploration leading to a markable
  conjecture or generalisation;
* **guided discovery:** linked prompts that expose a relationship before formalising
  it; and
* **synthesis:** combine concepts or representations in linked auto-markable parts.

Optional genuinely open tasks can sit outside auto-marked mastery as discussion,
teacher-led, or extension activities.

### P3 — make diagnostic intent enforceable

Add or require authoring metadata for:

* one primary target misconception per question;
* one wrong-method mapping per MCQ distractor; and
* a typed-answer wrong-method story where applicable.

Until the schema supports this, maintain a sidecar audit manifest keyed by question ID.
