### Misconception Coverage

Effectively Assessed:

* confusing vertices and edges
* counting repeated labels as new vertices
* treating degree as global size rather than incident edges
* choosing fewer edges instead of lower total weight
* confusing path, trail, and circuit
* assuming weighted implies directed
* traversing directed edges backwards
* misunderstanding Euler endpoint parity
* applying tree rules without structural reasoning

Still Missing or Under-Assessed:

* assuming every connected network has an Euler trail
* assuming any route that returns to start is automatically a trail
* misuse of accepted terminology hierarchy when multiple labels are technically true

## Final Lesson Scores

* Assessment Quality: 8/10

  Most questions now create real evidence of understanding, but one mastery item remains too procedural and a couple of recognition items are still weaker than the rest.

* Diagnostic Quality: 8/10

  The lesson now diagnoses several meaningful misconceptions well, especially in route classification, direction, weight, and Euler reasoning.

* Difficulty Calibration: 7/10

  Mostly solid, but `m2` is inflated and a few boundary items sit lower than their assigned band.

* Question Diversity: 8/10

  The lesson covers a healthy mix of interpretation, production, diagnosis, constraints, and synthesis, especially compared with a typical terminology lesson.

* Mastery Quality: 8/10

  The mastery section is broadly strong and clearly different from guided and independent practice, but it is held back by one reject-level procedural item.

* Markability Robustness: 8/10

  Generally strong, with only minor residual risk around equivalent wording and format for route or endpoint outputs.

## Final Verdict

PATCH

This lesson is fundamentally sound and much stronger than a typical terminology set, but it cannot receive `PASS` under the stated publication standard because:

* `REJECT` is not zero
* Difficulty Calibration is below 8/10

The minimum patch is to replace `y12s2-net-term-m2` with a genuinely inferential mastery task and, ideally, strengthen `g3` and `i3` so the low-level recognition layer is less exposed.
