## Systemic Issues

* Mastery Q1-Q7 is dominated by terminology recall and answer extraction rather than mathematical production.
* Difficulty is materially inflated: several D3-D4 items are actually D1, and all three D5 items are better calibrated at D3-D4.
* Guided, independent and mastery sections repeat the same edge-count, vertex-count, path and circuit tasks with only labels or numbers changed.
* Generic hints and explanations reduce diagnostic usefulness, especially on MCQ items.
* Degree questions accept angle-unit answers, creating false-positive marking.
* The strongest questions use degree parity, Euler-trail conditions and tree properties, but their answer formats or difficulty labels need adjustment.
* Context is often decorative; roads, schools and cables rarely change the mathematical decision required.

## Overall Lesson Quality

* Assessment Quality: 4/10

  The lesson checks basic vocabulary, but eight questions are not worth retaining in their current form and most mastery items do not assess mastery.

* Diagnostic Quality: 3/10

  A few questions target edge-versus-vertex, degree and parity misconceptions, but repeated extraction tasks reveal little about why a student is wrong.

* Difficulty Calibration: 2/10

  D3-D5 labels are repeatedly assigned to D1-D2 recognition and routine property application.

* Question Diversity: 3/10

  The set repeatedly counts edges or vertices and names paths, circuits, weights and directed edges. Only the final three questions introduce meaningfully different reasoning.

## Overall Finding

This lesson does not currently meet the Nova Maths Question Quality Standard. Its structure and answer fields are technically usable, but the educational value is too low across most of mastery. The minimum defensible remediation is to reject the eight extraction/duplicate items, patch the nine calibration or markability issues, and retain the two pass items with only explanation or distractor refinement.
